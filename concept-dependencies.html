Concept Dependencies

This page is a reading order for the course ideas. It shows which earlier concepts carry the load for later theorem-level ideas, and what question proves that the dependency has been understood.

How To Use This Map

Do not read the course as a flat list of terms. Later ideas rely on earlier agreements: what counts as the same space, which motions are legal, why signs mean anything, and how local data can add to a whole-surface demand. If a later theorem feels sudden, walk backward through the dependency row that supports it.

The right test is not whether a name is familiar. The test is whether the reader can say what the earlier idea lets the later idea do. A quotient lets edge labels become a surface. Orientation lets signs become evidence. Euler characteristic lets Poincare-Hopf speak about motion.

From local patch evidence to whole-surface behavior

A small patch can look ordinary while the whole surface changes what routes and sides mean. The reader has to inspect the completed object before trusting a conclusion drawn from one neighborhood.

Understand first: Topology and geometryBoundary and orientation

Then read: ManifoldQuotient space

Why this dependency matters: This is the first dependency in the course. Before a reader can trust later surface arguments, they need to see that local evidence is not enough. Quotient rules and manifold language give that local-versus-global gap a precise form by saying how ordinary patches are assembled into a whole object.

Reader check: Can I say what is locally ordinary, what whole-surface fact is still undecided, and which later claim would fail if I treated one small patch as the entire surface?

From legal motion to protected mathematical evidence in proof

A picture may move, but the proof needs a fact that moves with it unchanged. The dependency is the step from visual motion to accountable evidence that survives every legal stage.

Understand first: DeformationGeneric position

Then read: InvariantParity

Why this dependency matters: Deformation by itself is only motion. Generic position cleans the picture enough to inspect it. Invariant and parity add evidence: something survives the motion and can rule out a desired ending. The reader needs this order because a protected fact is what lets a simpler picture speak for the original one.

Reader check: What exact move is legal, what fact is still true after that move, and what tempting shortcut would make the final picture answer a different problem? Name the protected fact before trusting the simplified drawing.

From built spaces to durable surface classification rules

A space built from choices, identifications, or replacements can be sorted by durable surface features. The construction rule matters because it creates the routes, sides, boundaries, and pieces that later classification reads.

Understand first: Product spaceQuotient spaceSurgery

Then read: ManifoldBoundary and orientationEuler characteristic

Why this dependency matters: Products make choice-spaces, quotients make sameness rules, and surgery changes a surface under boundary control. Those operations prepare the reader to count faces, track orientation, and name the surface by what survives simplification. Classification depends on this because the finished surface is not just the first drawing; it is the object produced by the rule.

Reader check: What construction rule made this space, which surface features did that rule create or preserve, and what classification claim would change if the rule were changed? Say whether the rule affects sides, boundary, handles, or routes.

From surface ledger to stable whole-shape number

A surface count first looks like bookkeeping, then becomes a number that can control geometry and motion. The dependency is learning why a count belongs to the surface, not to one chosen mesh.

Understand first: Triangulation and cell decompositionEuler characteristic

Then read: Gauss-Bonnet as total turningPoincare-Hopf theorem

Why this dependency matters: Triangulation makes a soft surface finite enough to count. Euler characteristic shows that the final account does not belong to one mesh. Later, Gauss-Bonnet and Poincare-Hopf use that same whole-surface number to constrain turning and vector-field defects. Without that stability, the later theorems would compare local data to a number with no authority.

Reader check: Is this number attached to one drawing, or to the surface after redrawings and refinements? What local geometry or arrow-field evidence later has to answer to this whole-surface account?

From clean meetings to signed obstruction under deformation

Clean meetings can be assigned signs, and the signed total can force a later meeting to exist. The reader must first know when a meeting is ordinary enough to count and when signs have a shared direction rule.

Understand first: Generic positionBoundary and orientationWinding and linking

Then read: Intersection numberFixed points

Why this dependency matters: Generic position separates meetings. Orientation gives signs meaning. Winding and linking train the idea of a stable relation. Intersection number combines these into a count that survives legal motion, then fixed-point theory uses meetings between graph and diagonal. The dependency matters because a forced meeting is only convincing after accidental meetings have been separated from protected signed evidence.

Reader check: Are the meetings clean, do the signs come from a real direction rule, and what legal motion would create a canceling pair rather than change the protected total? Point to the local event being counted.

From a rule to forced self-agreement inside space

A rule becomes a shape in a larger space; self-agreement becomes a meeting with the diagonal. The dependency turns an abstract rule into a visible relation between input and output.

Understand first: Product spaceDual picturesIntersection number

Then read: Fixed pointsBrouwer fixed-point theorem

Why this dependency matters: Product space holds input and output together. Duality allows the rule to be redrawn as a graph. Intersection number explains why a graph may be unable to avoid the diagonal. Brouwer is the closed-ball version where the space itself blocks total escape. The reader needs this chain to see why a fixed point is forced self-agreement, not a guessed location.

Reader check: What is the graph, what is the diagonal, why would their meeting mean a fixed point, and which assumption keeps the rule from escaping the space? Separate existence of a meeting from finding its location.

From arrow-field failure to signed index around defects

A place where motion stops can be read by how nearby arrows turn around it. The dependency moves from naming a special point to reading the local arrow pattern that makes the point countable.

Understand first: Fixed pointsEquilibriumGeneric position

Then read: Vector field indexPoincare-Hopf theorem

Why this dependency matters: Fixed points teach forced special states. Equilibrium turns that idea into a failure of an arrow field. Generic position keeps failures isolated. Vector-field index makes each failure countable, and Poincare-Hopf adds those counts over the whole surface. The order matters because a dot alone is not evidence; the surrounding arrows supply the local signed account.

Reader check: Is the defect isolated, what do the arrows do on a small loop around it, and how will that local turning enter the whole-surface index total? Keep the dot, loop, sign, and surface account separate.

From state-space model to physical application constraints

A physical problem becomes usable only after its possible states are turned into the right mathematical space. The model must record complete states, remove impossible states, and translate paths back into legal motion.

Understand first: Configuration spaceFixed pointsPoincare-Hopf theorem

Then read: Dual picturesInvariantVector field index

Why this dependency matters: Configuration space lists the possible states. Fixed points and Poincare-Hopf supply forced-behavior tools. Duality and invariants keep the translation honest by asking whether the new picture still means the original physical question. This dependency matters because an application can sound clear while proving a theorem about the wrong state space.

Reader check: Does the state space include the real freedoms and exclude the real forbidden states? What physical conclusion would become unreliable if the model forgot a constraint or added a false freedom?
