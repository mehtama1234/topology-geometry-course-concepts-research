Lecture 12: Index becomes global bookkeeping

After defining the index of a vector field, the course asks what all the local indices know together. Can a surface force the total behavior of every vector field on it? The problem is to connect many small arrow failures to one number belonging to the whole surface.

Lecture Essay

Lecture 12 asks what all the local indices know together. A vector field may have several equilibria: sources, sinks, saddles, and other local arrow patterns. Each has an index, but those indices are not independent. When the field lives on a surface, the sum of the local indices is tied to the Euler characteristic of that surface. Local failures of motion add up to a whole-shape number.

Start by walking around a tiny loop surrounding one defect and watching the nearby arrows. The arrows may turn once with the loop, turn the other way, or combine in a more complicated local pattern. The index records that turning. Names such as source or saddle are secondary; the field has failed to give a nonzero direction at the center, and the surrounding arrows leave evidence of how that failure behaves.

The earlier surface bookkeeping now receives its dynamics job. Euler characteristic was not just a cell-counting curiosity. It becomes the number that controls the total defect of a vector field. The lecture therefore connects three layers of the course: surfaces can be decomposed and counted, vector fields can fail locally, and topology can force the total of those failures. Poincare-Hopf makes motion answer to shape by requiring the local defect account to match the whole-surface account.

Local defects can move around without making the total free. A source can be shifted, a positive-negative pair can be created or canceled under the right circumstances, and a drawing can be cleaned up. But the total signed index is not free when the underlying surface is fixed. That is the same survival principle from intersection number, now expressed in the language of dynamics. The arithmetic was designed to ignore harmless local reshuffling and remember the whole-surface demand.

For the reader, this lecture is the moment when Euler characteristic stops being only a surface label. It becomes a demand placed on motion. The same number obtained from cutting a surface into pieces now says how arrow-field defects must add up on that surface. That is the big conceptual turn: a number first learned from shape can later constrain every continuous choice of arrows on that shape.

Lecture Deepening

What is really happening: The lecture asks what all local vector-field defects know together when their signed indices are added over the full surface carrying the arrows as one account. The surface, not one favorite defect, decides what the total is allowed to be.

Why it is hard: It is easy to focus on one source, sink, or saddle and miss the completed ledger: every isolated defect on the same surface has to enter the total. The theorem speaks about the full defect account, not a favorite local picture.

Key move: Clean the field to isolated defects, assign each local index, then compare the sum with the surface carrying the field rather than one defect alone. The cleanup makes the local failures countable before the whole-surface comparison is trusted.

Payoff: Euler characteristic becomes a demand on motion, not only a surface bookkeeping number from earlier classification work on static shapes and decompositions. Surface accounting and arrow-defect accounting begin to speak the same language.

First Principles

A vector field may have many defects. Some look like sources, sinks, saddles, or rotating patterns. Each defect has a local signed count. The surprise is that the total of those local counts is not chosen freely by the field. The surface itself controls it.

The Mathematical Move

Add the indices of all equilibria and compare the sum with a whole-surface number. This is the road to Poincare-Hopf: local arrow failures add up to Euler characteristic. The theorem works because local defects can move or split, but their signed total is tied to the surface.

The Important Detail

The theorem counts isolated equilibria under clean conditions. If equilibria smear into a whole curve or captions garble a condition, the safe interpretation is the first-principles one: clean up to a generic case, then count.

How It Connects

This lecture binds Euler-style surface bookkeeping to vector-field behavior. It makes the old count of cells matter for motion. The same alternating count that once described a surface now predicts what any arrow pattern on that surface must fail to do.
