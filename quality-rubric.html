Quality Rubric

This page is the writing standard for the companion. It catches prose that is long but still too loose by requiring every important explanation to name the object, move, surviving evidence, failure condition, course anchor, and plain-language replacement.

How To Use The Rubric

Read a lecture, concept, theme, subtheme, or method-family page with these six tests beside it. If a paragraph cannot pass one test, the repair is not to add a harder word. The repair is to state the missing object, action, protected fact, failure condition, source footing, or everyday sentence.

The goal is not simpler in the sense of thinner. The goal is more accountable. The page should make its work clear before it asks for trust in a theorem name, formula, or formal term.

object-before-name

Name the object before the term

Test: The page must say what concrete thing is being reasoned about before leaning on the mathematical name: a surface, path, field, map, route, count, meeting, or state space. The object has to carry information the proof will use, not merely sit beside the formal term.

Complete answer: A complete explanation lets a reader point to the object and say what information it carries. The term can then shorten the sentence, but it cannot replace the sentence. For example, 'quotient space' is acceptable only after the page says which points are being identified and what travel rule changes in the finished shape.

Failure: The writing fails when a reader can repeat the title but cannot say what thing is being moved, counted, glued, compared, or forced. A title-only paragraph feels fluent but gives the reader no object to inspect, no data to protect, and no way to check the later claim.

Repair: Rewrite the opening around the object in the lecture or concept. Say what is present, what data it carries, and why that object is the right stage for the problem. Then let the mathematical name arrive as a label for that already visible job.

legal-move

Name the legal move

Test: The page must say what action is allowed: deforming, cutting, gluing, counting with signs, comparing a graph with a diagonal, walking around a defect, or building a state space. The action must include its rulebook, because the same visible motion can be legal in one problem and false in another.

Complete answer: A complete explanation says what changes and what is forbidden to change. It treats the allowed move as the main proof obligation, not as a background detail. It identifies the exact moment where an illegal crossing, moved boundary, changed gluing rule, or dishonest model would stop the argument.

Failure: The writing fails when it says the picture is simplified, transformed, or represented without saying which changes still answer the original problem. That failure is serious because a cleaner picture may belong to a different problem, even if it looks easier to reason about.

Repair: Add the contract of motion or translation. State one allowed action and one action that would change the problem instead of solving it. Tie both to the object: what is allowed to move, what must stay fixed, and what evidence the move is supposed to preserve.

protected-fact

Name what survives

Test: The page must identify the durable evidence: parity, Euler characteristic, boundary order, orientation, signed total, fixed-point meeting, index sum, or another preserved relation. The evidence has to be something that survives the allowed move and still has enough force to support the conclusion.

Complete answer: A complete explanation makes the protected fact do work. The conclusion follows because that fact survived the legal move, not because the page says the method is useful. It shows the chain: this fact stayed intact, therefore this escape route is blocked or this existence claim is forced.

Failure: The writing fails when it gives an outcome without showing what evidence survived long enough to force that outcome. A conclusion that appears after a theorem name but before a protected fact is only an announcement, not an explanation.

Repair: Add one sentence that starts with the surviving evidence and ends with the conclusion it can force. If the evidence is a count, say why the count survives the local threat. If the evidence is a meeting or boundary rule, say why legal motion cannot erase it.

failure-condition

Say what would break the claim

Test: The page must name the hidden condition that would make the explanation invalid: missing orientation, illegal crossing, changed boundary, incomplete defect list, wrong state space, or overread source. The condition should be stated in ordinary terms before any formal assumption is trusted.

Complete answer: A complete explanation protects the reader from using the idea too broadly. It names the condition under which the same words would no longer prove the same claim. That condition is part of the mathematics, because it marks where the method stops speaking and where a tempting overclaim begins.

Failure: The writing fails when every method sounds available everywhere and no page says when the method stops helping. This produces the worst kind of shallow depth: the paragraph has many correct terms, but no boundary on where those terms are allowed to work.

Repair: Add a failure sentence tied to the object. Say what assumption is required and what would go wrong if the assumption were absent. Make the break visible in a concrete way: a sign cannot be assigned, a path crosses what it may not cross, a defect is missing, or a model includes an impossible state.

course-anchor

Tie the idea to a course moment

Test: The page must connect the explanation to a lecture moment, source trail, example, or transcript-grounded anchor rather than floating as a general summary. The anchor should affect the explanation itself: it should show which object, move, or source limit is actually being used.

Complete answer: A complete explanation lets the reader move from the page back to a lecture example or source note and see why that exact moment supports the claim. The course anchor is evidence, not an add-on; it keeps the companion from becoming a generic topology summary detached from Tokieda's sequence.

Failure: The writing fails when it could be pasted into any topology summary without changing a word. It also fails when the page names a lecture but does not use that lecture to explain the object, move, protected fact, or source boundary.

Repair: Add the lecture number, example, or source caveat that makes the explanation belong to this course companion. Then connect the anchor to the reasoning: what did the lecture make visible, what did the source support, and what must not be claimed from that evidence?

plain-language-replacement

Replace formal words with everyday sentences

Test: The page must be readable by someone who has not studied topology. Formal words may appear, but each important word needs a plain sentence saying what job it performs. The plain sentence should explain the work of the idea, not merely swap in another formal synonym.

Complete answer: A complete explanation can be read twice: first as everyday reasoning about shapes and motion, then as the formal mathematical term that names that reasoning. The job comes before the label: keeping track, blocking escape, recording a turn, carrying signs, or listing possible states.

Failure: The writing fails when it hides the reason inside words such as invariant, manifold, index, orientation, generic position, or configuration space. It also fails when the replacement sentence is vague, for example saying an idea is powerful or important without saying what it lets the proof do.

Repair: After each important word, add a sentence beginning with the job it performs: it keeps track, blocks an escape, records a turn, carries signs, or lists possible states. Then test that sentence by removing the formal word; the paragraph should still explain the mathematical move.

Completion Test

A page is not ready merely because it is long. It is ready when a reader can use the page to answer six questions: What is the object? What move is allowed? What fact survives? What would break the claim? Which course moment or source supports it? What everyday sentence replaces the formal word?
