Repair Clinic

This page teaches the reviewer to hear the difference between fluent but shallow prose and a real first-principles explanation. Each case starts with a weak sentence, names the missing mathematical work, and rewrites the idea so a beginner can audit it.

How To Use A Repair Case

Read the flawed explanation first and ask why it feels acceptable. Most weak explanations contain a correct word or memorable phrase, but they skip the object, legal move, protected fact, source footing, or failure condition. The repair move names the missing obligation.

The strong version is not meant to sound formal. It is stronger because it says what is being studied, what is allowed to change, what survives, and what conclusion that survival can support. Use the reviewer test to decide whether the repaired sentence now carries the course idea.

Repair case

Mobius strip as a visual surprise

Flawed explanation: The Mobius strip is surprising because it has one side, so topology is about weird shapes.

Why it fails: This sentence remembers the punchline but loses the method. It does not say what object is being tested, what motion or walk reveals the fact, or why local patches fail to show the whole behavior.

Repair move: Replace the surprise with the walk-around account. Say what a mark or traveler does on the strip and what returns changed after one complete trip.

Strong version: A Mobius strip matters because a local view of the paper does not reveal the whole-side behavior. When a mark is followed around the full strip, the route returns with side information that an ordinary band would not have. The course uses this to show why whole-shape facts can be invisible in small pieces.

Reviewer test: Can the reader explain the one-side claim by describing a full trip around the strip rather than repeating the phrase?

Repair case

Invariant as any number that stays useful

Flawed explanation: An invariant is an important number we calculate from a shape, and it helps us compare examples.

Why it fails: This sounds plausible but avoids the main test. A number is not an invariant because it is useful once; it earns the role by surviving the allowed changes in the problem.

Repair move: Name the legal move that threatens the count, then explain why the proposed number survives that move or how signs and cancellation repair the count.

Strong version: An invariant is evidence designed to survive the moves the problem allows. If two crossings are born as an opposite pair, a raw crossing count changes, but a signed total can stay fixed. The invariant is useful because it carries the same information from one legal drawing to another.

Reviewer test: Does the explanation say which allowed change tries to alter the number and why the chosen account survives?

Repair case

Quotient space as a square with arrows

Flawed explanation: A quotient space is shown by drawing a square with arrows on the sides.

Why it fails: The sentence mistakes the code for the object. The arrows are not decoration on a square; they are instructions that decide which points become the same point in the finished space.

Repair move: State the identification rule before reasoning from the picture. Then say what route, boundary, or side behavior changes after the rule is applied.

Strong version: A quotient-space drawing is a set of instructions. The square is only a readable way to say which edge points are identified. After the identifications are made, some apparent borders may disappear and the routes on the finished surface may differ from routes on the flat drawing.

Reviewer test: Can the reader say which points are being declared identical and what claim changes after that declaration?

Repair case

Generic position as making the drawing nicer

Flawed explanation: Generic position means we move the picture slightly so it looks nicer and avoids special cases.

Why it fails: This treats generic position as visual cleanup. The course uses the small move to create ordinary events that can be counted, signed, or isolated without hidden coincidences.

Repair move: Say what accident is removed and what evidence becomes available afterward: clean crossings, separated events, isolated defects, or a defined sign rule.

Strong version: Generic position is a way to replace a fragile exact accident with an ordinary case that can carry evidence. After a tiny legal nudge, crossings can be separated, defects can be isolated, and signs or counts can be assigned. The point is not a nicer picture; it is a picture whose events can be audited.

Reviewer test: Does the explanation identify the removed accident and the count or sign rule that becomes possible?

Repair case

Fixed point as a point that does not move

Flawed explanation: A fixed point is a point that does not move, so fixed-point theorems tell us where something stays.

Why it fails: The first phrase is only a start, and the second phrase is too strong. The course needs a rule from a space to itself, and the theorem often proves existence without locating the point.

Repair move: Name the domain and the rule, then translate the condition into self-agreement: the input and output are the same point.

Strong version: A fixed point belongs to a rule that sends points of a space back into the same space. A point is fixed when the rule returns that exact point. The graph-and-diagonal picture turns this self-agreement into a meeting question, and a theorem may prove that some meeting exists without computing where it is.

Reviewer test: Can the reader identify the rule, the space it maps into itself, and the difference between existence and location?

Repair case

Index as the number of equilibria

Flawed explanation: The index counts how many equilibrium points a vector field has, so more equilibria means a larger index.

Why it fails: This collapses a signed local account into a raw count. Index is assigned around an isolated defect by watching how arrows turn, and different defects can contribute different signed amounts.

Repair move: Replace the raw count with the walk-around test. Say what loop encloses the defect and how arrows turn during one full circuit.

Strong version: Vector-field index is local signed evidence around one isolated defect. To read it, walk around a small loop containing that defect and watch how nearby arrows turn. Later, those local indices can be added and compared with a whole-surface number, but one index is not merely one equilibrium counted once.

Reviewer test: Does the explanation separate the number of defects from the signed turning account around each defect?

Repair case

Configuration space as a diagram of the machine

Flawed explanation: A configuration space is a picture of all the ways a machine can move.

Why it fails: The sentence gestures in the right direction but stays too loose. A configuration space is not just a picture; one point must represent one complete allowed state, and forbidden states must be removed.

Repair move: State what one point of the space represents, which freedoms vary, and which states are excluded before using topology to reason about motion.

Strong version: A configuration space records complete allowed states. For a moving system, one point might record every position or joint angle needed to describe the system at one instant. Collisions, blocked positions, or impossible choices must be removed, because paths in this space are supposed to represent real legal motions.

Reviewer test: Can the reader say what one point represents and name at least one forbidden state that must not be included?

Repair case

Poincare-Hopf as topology predicting motion

Flawed explanation: Poincare-Hopf says topology predicts how a dynamical system moves, so the theorem tells us the motion path.

Why it fails: The sentence is too broad. The theorem compares the sum of local indices of isolated defects with the Euler characteristic of the same surface. It constrains defects; it does not solve the whole motion law.

Repair move: Name the surface, the vector field, the isolated defects, the local indices, and the whole-surface number being compared.

Strong version: Poincare-Hopf connects local failures of a vector field to the whole surface carrying that field. Each isolated defect has an index, and the sum of all those indices must match the Euler characteristic of the same surface. This can force defects or rule out some fields, but it does not compute every trajectory.

Reviewer test: Does the explanation include both sides of the contract: all local indices and the Euler characteristic of the same surface?

The Repair Test

A repair is finished only when the explanation can be checked by a reader who does not already know the formal term. If the reader can name the object, the move, the protected fact, and the failure condition in everyday language, the repair has done its job.

Use the clinic in two passes. First, mark the missing obligation: object, move, evidence, failure, course anchor, or everyday sentence. Second, rewrite only that missing part and leave the rest alone. A good repair is usually a precise added sentence, not a louder restatement of the same claim.

The strongest repairs also say what they refuse to prove. A fixed-point theorem may prove existence without location. Poincare-Hopf may constrain defects without solving paths. A quotient drawing may define a surface without being the surface itself.

If the refusal is missing, the repaired sentence may still overclaim even when every word sounds simple and friendly to the reader.
