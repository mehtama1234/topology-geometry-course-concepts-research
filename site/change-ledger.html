Change Ledger

This page makes the central habit of the course explicit: before trusting a picture, count, model, theorem, or source sentence, separate what is allowed to change from what must stay fixed.

How To Read The Ledger

Each row begins with the object. That matters because beginners often start with the visible picture instead of the mathematical thing being studied. The object may be a strip with a gluing rule, a route with obstacles, a cell account of a surface, a map from a space to itself, a vector field, a state space, or a source claim.

The legal-change line states the permission. Some motion, redrawing, refinement, or weakening is allowed, but only inside a contract. The protected-fact line states the evidence that must survive that permission. The why-it-matters line says what mathematical work the surviving evidence can do.

The false move is included because many shallow explanations sound acceptable until the illegal change is named. The reader test is the spoken checkpoint: if the answer cannot say what changes and what stays in everyday language, the formal term has arrived too early.

Change case

Paper strip to global surface fact

Object: The object is the whole strip with its end-gluing rule, not the small rectangular patch held in the hand.

Legal change: The strip may be bent, carried, or locally inspected as long as the end relation and the route being followed are not changed.

Protected fact: The protected fact is whether a trip around the strip returns to the same side or to the opposite side.

Why it matters: That surviving side information is the first lesson of the course: a small local view can miss a whole-shape fact that controls what later cuts and routes can do.

False move: The false move is to flatten a twisted strip into an ordinary band and then reason as if the gluing rule had survived.

Reader test: Can the reader say which part of the story is allowed to bend and which part must remember the original end relation?

Change case

Messy route to legal deformation

Object: The object is a route or curve drawn with endpoint, boundary, obstacle, or crossing rules attached to it.

Legal change: The route may be nudged into a cleaner position when endpoints, forbidden regions, and required separations remain respected during the entire motion.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the route's relation to the obstacle, boundary order, or other curve that the problem refuses to let it cross illegally.

Why it matters: A deformation proof works because it replaces a hard picture by an easier picture without changing the question being answered.

False move: The false move is to clean the drawing by sliding through the obstacle or through another curve and then call the final picture equivalent.

Reader test: Can the reader name one motion that is legal, one motion that is forbidden, and the exact fact that survived the legal motion?

Change case

Visible crossings to signed evidence

Object: The object is a pair of curves or sheets whose meetings are being counted after they have been put in ordinary position.

Legal change: The curves may move through legal deformations that create or remove simple meeting pairs, provided the boundary and orientation rules are preserved.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the signed total, because opposite-sign pairs can be born or die together while the total remains accountable.

Why it matters: The course trusts signed evidence because it survives the local accidents that would make a raw crossing count untrustworthy.

False move: The false move is to count every visible crossing equally and forget that a small legal motion can create two crossings that should cancel.

Reader test: Can the reader describe the local event, the two signs it creates, and why the useful total does not change?

Change case

Cell drawing to Euler characteristic

Object: The object is a surface cut into vertices, edges, and faces so the whole surface can be counted.

Legal change: The cutting pattern may be refined, redrawn, or simplified as long as it still describes the same surface and respects the boundary.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the alternating count of vertices, edges, and faces, not any one chosen drawing of the cells.

Why it matters: Euler characteristic matters because it turns the whole surface into a number that survives changes in the bookkeeping plan.

False move: The false move is to treat a convenient cell drawing as the surface itself and trust a count without checking whether the same surface is still being counted.

Reader test: Can the reader say what changes when the cutting pattern changes and what number is supposed to remain the same?

Change case

Square diagram to quotient surface

Object: The object is the finished surface defined by edge identifications, not the square sheet before those identifications are imposed.

Legal change: The diagram may be redrawn or relabeled only when the same points are still being identified in the finished space.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the identification rule: which edges, directions, and boundary points become the same point of the surface.

Why it matters: Quotient thinking matters because it lets the course build surfaces whose global behavior is not visible from the flat drawing alone.

False move: The false move is to reason about distances or sides in the square as if no points had been glued together.

Reader test: Can the reader point to two different drawn points and say whether they become one point after the quotient rule is applied?

Change case

Map rule to fixed-point evidence

Object: The object is a rule that sends each point of a space back into the same space, together with the graph of that rule.

Legal change: The rule may be studied through a graph-and-diagonal comparison when continuity and the same-domain same-target condition are preserved.

Protected fact: The protected fact is forced self-agreement: a meeting between the graph and the diagonal means a point is sent to itself.

Why it matters: This matters because existence can be forced by shape even when the exact fixed point is not calculated.

False move: The false move is to say a fixed point exists before checking that the rule actually maps the space into itself in the required way.

Reader test: Can the reader identify the space, the rule, the diagonal of self-agreement, and what the theorem can and cannot locate?

Change case

Arrow field to local index

Object: The object is a vector field on a surface, especially what the arrows do near an isolated place where the field fails.

Legal change: The arrows may be deformed away from the defect as long as the boundary loop around the defect keeps its comparison to direction.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the turning number of the arrows around the defect, counted with sign.

Why it matters: Index matters because it changes a local failure of motion into evidence that can be added across the whole surface.

False move: The false move is to count visible equilibria while ignoring whether their local arrow patterns have the same signed behavior.

Reader test: Can the reader walk around one defect and explain what is being counted before adding it to other defects?

Change case

Local defects to Poincare-Hopf

Object: The object is one vector field on one surface, with all isolated defects included in the same account.

Legal change: Defects may move or split under permitted changes to the field, but the comparison must stay on the same surface.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the total signed index of all defects, which must match the Euler characteristic under the theorem's assumptions.

Why it matters: The theorem matters because it ties every local failure of the field to a whole-surface number that cannot be negotiated away.

False move: The false move is to use the theorem as if it describes every motion path, rather than a constraint on the sum of local indices.

Reader test: Can the reader name both sides of the equality and say why leaving out one defect would break the account?

Change case

Machine motion to configuration space

Object: The object is the space of allowed states, where one point records one complete position or choice of the system.

Legal change: A physical motion becomes a path only if every intermediate state is allowed and all forbidden collisions or blocked positions have been removed.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the shape of the allowed state space: its walls, holes, separated regions, and possible paths.

Why it matters: Configuration space matters because it changes a moving physical story into a static shape that can carry obstructions.

False move: The false move is to draw the machine itself and assume the drawing records all freedoms, obstacles, and continuous choices.

Reader test: Can the reader say what one point records, which states are forbidden, and what a path in the space means physically?

Change case

Source sentence to supported claim

Object: The object is a claim in the companion, together with the caption, lecture context, paper, or textbook support available for it.

Legal change: The sentence may be strengthened or weakened only according to the evidence actually available and the source caveats around that evidence.

Protected fact: The protected fact is the boundary between caption support, course-arc inference, background source support, and claims that still need human checking.

Why it matters: Source discipline matters because mathematical explanation should not become louder than the evidence, especially with auto-captions and one missing caption file.

False move: The false move is to treat a reference link as proof that every surrounding explanatory sentence was stated in the lecture.

Reader test: Can the reader quote the exact claim, name the evidence behind it, and rewrite the sentence if the evidence is weaker?

The Ledger Test

Use this page when an explanation feels fluent but unearned. Ask four questions in order. What is the object? What is allowed to change? What fact is protected while that change happens? What conclusion becomes possible because the fact was protected?

If the answer skips the second question, it may be using an illegal simplification. If it skips the third, it may be giving a visual story without evidence. If it skips the fourth, it may name a correct invariant or theorem without explaining why the reader should care.

The same test applies to sources. A sentence can change when new evidence is found, but the boundary between caption support, course inference, and background source support must remain visible. That boundary is part of the mathematics because it keeps the explanation accountable.

Repairing A Weak Answer

When an answer fails the ledger test, repair only the missing part. If the object is vague, add one sentence that says what one point, route, field, cell, or glued edge represents. If the legal change is vague, add one sentence naming the allowed motion and the forbidden shortcut. If the protected fact is vague, add one sentence naming the count, side information, equality, path question, or source boundary that survives.

A repaired answer should not become more formal just to sound safer. It should become more checkable. The reader should be able to point to the object, act out the allowed change, say what would break the argument, and name the conclusion that the protected fact can support. That is the course habit in its plainest form.

Give no credit for a sentence that only says two pictures are the same, a count is invariant, a theorem applies, or a source supports the claim. Give credit when the sentence explains the permission behind that statement. The permission is the part a beginner can test.
