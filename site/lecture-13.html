First Principles

The theorem says that every isolated defect in one vector field on one surface belongs to a complete signed account. Add the index of each defect, then compare that total with the Euler characteristic of the same surface. On a sphere, this explains why a continuous nonzero tangent arrow pattern cannot exist everywhere. The surface demands a total defect account.

The Mathematical Move

Use the equation between total index and Euler characteristic as a two-way tool. Known topology predicts forced defects; known local indices can diagnose topology. The same equality can answer different questions depending on which side of the equation is already understood, but it does not solve the motion path.

The Important Detail

This is not about drawing a tidy arrow field. It is about the impossibility of making all local arrow choices agree with the whole surface. A sphere, for example, does not give the arrows enough global freedom to avoid defects everywhere.
