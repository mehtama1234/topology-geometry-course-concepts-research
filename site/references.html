Henri Poincare, Analysis Situs and its supplements

Why it belongs: These papers sit in the historical source line for topology as the study of spaces through preserved structure. For this companion, their job is to anchor the whole-space question: which facts belong to the completed carrier rather than to one drawing? They support the course's move from local pieces and drawings toward invariants, manifolds, and whole-surface accounts.

Use carefully: They are not course lecture notes and they are not evidence that Tokieda cited a specific sentence. Use them after the course object is plain: surface, allowed change, preserved account, and claim boundary. The page should translate their role back into everyday course jobs before any historical name or original notation appears.

primary paper

Heinz Hopf, Vektorfelder in n-dimensionalen Mannigfaltigkeiten

Why it belongs: Hopf's paper is a primary reference for the vector-field-index thread behind Poincare-Hopf: local singularities of a field are added and compared with a number belonging to the whole manifold. It gives the source trail for the course's later idea that local failures of motion are not independent once the carrier surface is fixed.

Use carefully: The paper is technical and historically downstream from earlier surface intuition. The companion should first explain the arrow field, isolated defects, local turning, and same-surface total before pointing readers to the paper. Do not let the reference turn the lecture page into a formal-paper summary or a slogan about one equilibrium.

Use carefully: It is broader and more algebraic than Tokieda's course. Link it only after the lecture page has already explained the route, gluing rule, deformation, or count in everyday terms. standard reference

John Milnor, Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint

Why it belongs: Milnor's notes connect manifolds, degree, fixed points, and vector-field ideas in a compact differentiable-topology setting. For this companion, they clarify why prepared local conditions can carry whole-space conclusions. They support the bridge from smooth local behavior to topological existence or counting claims without forcing the reader to start from formal prerequisites.

Use carefully: The text assumes more mathematical preparation than the companion. Use it only after the everyday explanation has named the object, legal move, local condition, and global count or existence claim. Its role is formal backup for a prepared idea, not a substitute route that lets the page skip the plain-language setup.

Victor Guillemin and Alan Pollack, Differential Topology

Why it belongs: This reference explains generic position, transversality-style thinking, intersection counts, and the route from local smooth data to global topological conclusions. Its course-facing job is to justify the cleanup before signs are trusted. It supports the moment when a visible meeting stops being a sketch feature and becomes evidence that can survive legal nudges.

Use carefully: It belongs in the reference layer because it is more formal than the lecture treatment. The site should first say what meeting was cleaned, what sign rule became available, and why the count survives.
