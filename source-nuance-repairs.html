Source Nuance Repairs

This page turns the source caveat into a revision tool. Auto-captions can preserve the broad lecture motion while distorting short mathematical words, names, or the strength of a claim. Each card says how to keep the explanation tied to what the source can support.

How To Use A Repair Note

Read the caption hazard before revising a lecture page. Then compare the safe claim with the stronger sentence you want to write. If the stronger sentence depends on a missing caption, a garbled term, or an inference from the course arc, use the repair move instead of pretending the source proves more than it does.

The do-not-claim line is a guardrail for plain language. It keeps everyday wording honest: the page should be simple enough for a beginner, but still careful about what was actually supported by recovered captions and visible course structure.

Lecture 01

The Mobius strip reveals the course's central problem

Caption hazard: The first lecture uses paper, color, cutting, and side language. Captions can make that sound like a craft instruction rather than a claim about what a full trip around the strip preserves.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the Mobius strip introduces the course habit: start with a concrete object, move through it carefully, and watch a whole-object fact that local patches hide.

Repair move: If a sentence says only that the strip has one side, add the walk-around test: the reader should imagine carrying a mark around the strip and returning changed.

Do not claim: Do not claim that all later topology ignores measurement. The first lecture separates side behavior from exact shape; it does not make geometry irrelevant.

Reviewer question: Can the reader explain what observation is made by walking around the strip, not just repeat the phrase one side?

Lecture 02

Deformation as a way to solve, not decorate

Caption hazard: Disk routing language can sound like a drawing puzzle. The caption may preserve the words curve and intersect without making clear that the proof is about all legal drawings.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the lecture turns a routing question into a deformation question: can the requested connections be changed without crossing under the stated rules?

Repair move: When a paragraph says a connection is impossible, add the protected feature that defeats every legal attempt, not only the failed picture shown first.

Do not claim: Do not treat one crowded drawing as the proof. The proof needs a reason that survives any allowed redrawing of the paths.

Reviewer question: Does the explanation name the endpoints, the allowed motion of paths, and the crossing condition that cannot be escaped?

Lecture 03

Building spaces from simple pieces

Caption hazard: Words such as product, quotient, surgery, and manifold can be misheard as labels for finished objects. The source risk is losing the construction rule that makes the object.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the lecture teaches how new spaces are built from simpler pieces and why the building rule matters more than the look of the drawing.

Repair move: If a sentence names a space, add how it was made: which pieces are paired, glued, removed, thickened, or treated as one point.

Do not claim: Do not let product or quotient appear as unexplained vocabulary. A beginner should see the choices recorded or the identifications imposed.

Reviewer question: Can the reader reconstruct the object from the construction rule without already knowing the formal name?

Lecture 05

Classifying surfaces by what survives cutting and gluing

Caption hazard: Classification, orientable, handle, and boundary can sound like a naming exercise. The course point is that cutting and gluing leave a small set of surviving surface features.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the lecture organizes surfaces by features that remain after legal rearrangement, especially handles, boundary pieces, and orientation behavior.

Moving subspaces and meeting only when dimension forces it

Lecture 08

Intersection number is a count with memory

Caption hazard: Positive and negative intersection words can be copied as signs without explaining where signs come from. The source risk is arithmetic without orientation.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the lecture turns intersection count into protected evidence by assigning signs so accidental births and deaths cancel.

Repair move: When a page uses plus and minus intersections, add the orientation or local direction rule that gives those signs meaning.

Do not claim: Do not call the raw number of crossings invariant. The signed total is the candidate that survives the legal deformation.

Reviewer question: Does the explanation show why a newly born pair contributes one positive and one negative meeting?

Lecture 10

Brouwer fixed point theorem: shape forces a point to stay

Caption hazard: Brouwer, closed ball, boundary, and continuous can make the theorem sound like a slogan. The reader needs the domain, rule, and no-escape condition before the name.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the lecture explains why a continuous self-map of a closed ball must leave some point fixed.

Repair move: When Brouwer is named, add the four checks: closed ball, map back into itself, continuity, and fixed-point conclusion without claiming a location.

Do not claim: Do not use Brouwer as a general statement that every motion has a fixed point. The object and rule are part of the theorem.

Reviewer question: Does the explanation say what space maps to itself and why the conclusion is existence rather than computation?

Vector fields: learning without solving the equations

Caption hazard: Vector field, equilibrium, differential equation, and index can collapse into one motion vocabulary. The lecture separates the arrows, the vanishing points, and what can be learned without solving.

Safe claim: It is safe to say the lecture introduces vector fields as a way to study motion by reading arrows and defects instead of solving every path.

Applications show the same method in new settings

The Source Repair Test

A lecture explanation is source-faithful when a reviewer can separate three things: what the recovered captions directly support, what the course arc reasonably suggests, and what still needs human checking in the original video. If those three are mixed together, the repair is not finished.
