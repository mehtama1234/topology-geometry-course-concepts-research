Term Translator

This page turns loaded course words into the work they do inside an argument. Read the everyday sentence first, then the formal word. The word can carry the argument only after the object, move, protected fact, and failure condition are visible.

How To Use This Page

When a page uses a word such as invariant, quotient space, manifold, index, or configuration space, pause and ask what job the word is doing. Is it naming the object, the allowed move, the surviving evidence, the sign rule, the state model, or the theorem that compares local and whole-shape facts?

This is a reading tool, not a word list. A formal word can shorten an idea only after that idea has already been explained. If the word arrives before its job is clear, use the reader question on the card to recover the missing sentence.

Formal word

Topology

Everyday sentence: Topology asks what a shape still makes possible after distances, angles, and exact measurements are allowed to change. It treats the drawing as flexible, but it keeps the rules about routes, boundaries, crossings, sides, and holes honest. The course reading rule is to make the ordinary job visible first, then let the formal name shorten later sentences. The reader can retell it without using the term at all.

Job in the argument: Its job is to move attention from measured appearance to permissions: which routes remain open, which crossings are forbidden, which boundaries matter, and which relationships survive legal motion. The proof uses those permissions to replace one picture with another without losing the original question. The job sentence names what evidence the term protects and what conclusion that evidence can support. The job has to be stated as something the argument does, not as a topic the page mentions.

Not a definition: It is not a license to ignore detail. It ignores only the details that the problem has declared irrelevant while protecting the detail that can force the answer. A topological explanation still needs a rulebook: what may move, what may not move, and what evidence survives the motion. A good warning names the overreach before it becomes part of the proof. This keeps the companion simple without letting simple wording become loose wording or letting a remembered label replace checked evidence.

Failure if misread: If topology is read as loose shape talk, the proof loses its rulebook. A reader may bend, cross, or erase a feature that the original problem required them to keep, then mistake a cleaner-looking new picture for evidence about the old problem. In another field, the matching failure appears when the object, allowed change, or protected evidence has not been rebuilt. The repaired reading names the condition, the evidence, and the smaller claim that remains safe.

Reader question: What exact detail is topology keeping alive after the drawing has been changed, which move would destroy that detail, and why does the remaining detail still have enough force to answer the problem? If the answer cannot be stated plainly, the term is being remembered rather than understood. Poincare-Hopf theorem

It connects every local failure of the field to one whole-surface ledger, so one defect cannot be read in isolation. This keeps the term from becoming a password: the reader can say what is present, what changes, and what has to stay true. The reader can retell it without using the term at all.

Job in the argument: Its job is to connect local motion failures with whole-shape bookkeeping. Defects can move or split, but their total has to respect the surface that carries the field. The theorem turns every local index into an entry in one global account. The reader can ask what would fail if this job were not done. The job has to be stated as something the argument does, not as a topic the page mentions.

Not a definition: It is not a statement about one equilibrium by itself. It is a statement about the total account across the entire surface. The theorem needs the right surface, the right boundary behavior, and every isolated defect in the same account. The non-definition tells the reader which tempting shortcut to refuse. This keeps the companion simple without letting simple wording become loose wording or letting a remembered label replace checked evidence.

Failure if misread: If the surface, boundary behavior, or defect list is wrong, the sum may no longer be the theorem's sum. The conclusion then overreads the model by comparing a partial or misplaced defect account with the wrong surface number. The failure case teaches the edge of the idea: this is where the same word stops being safe. The repaired reading names the condition, the evidence, and the smaller claim that remains safe.

Reader question: What are all the defects, what is their signed total, what surface number must that total match, what conclusion is not being computed, and which omitted defect or boundary condition would break the claim? Answer in everyday words first, then add the formal term only if it shortens an explanation that already works. Use the linked concepts, poincare-hopf, vector-field-index, euler-characteristic, only after the answer has already explained their ordinary work and named the claim limit.

The Common Test

For any term, ask what it lets the proof do that ordinary description could not do cleanly. A manifold lets local patches support a global question. An invariant carries evidence through legal motion. A configuration space turns motion into routes. An index turns a local arrow failure into a signed count. The word earns its place only when that work is visible.
